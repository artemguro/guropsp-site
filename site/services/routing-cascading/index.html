How it works

Three mechanisms that raise approval rates together.

01

Routing

Picks the first provider for a deposit by geo, method, BIN, issuing bank, amount and player history.

02

Cascading

Retries a declined deposit at another provider, but only for declines that are worth retrying.

03

Health control

If a provider’s approval rate dips or errors spike, traffic is redistributed automatically.

Routing rules

What the scheme is built from for your geos and providers.

01

Geo and currency

Each market gets its own provider set: one PSP performs better in Canada, another in Germany.

02

BIN and issuing bank

The same bank approves differently at different acquirers. We route by actual approval rates.

03

Deposit amount

Large deposits go to the provider with the best approval on high amounts, small ones to the cheaper one.

04

Sticky routing

A returning player’s deposit goes where the previous one was approved: the bank already “knows” the merchant.

05

Provider health

A drop in approval rate or a rise in technical errors lowers the provider’s weight until it recovers.

06

Price

With equal approval rates, traffic goes to the provider with the lower fee.

Why you can’t cascade everything

Some declines are recovered by cascading, some are pointless to retry, and some must not be retried under card scheme rules. Where the line falls depends on your providers, banks and data.

The same decline code is interpreted differently by Visa, Mastercard and individual acquirers, and the schemes limit retries and charge fees for violations. That’s why there is no universal “what to cascade” table: we build the matrix from your statistics, providers and issuing banks, and review it with you.

Cascading after 3DS

If a player has already passed 3DS and the provider declines the payment, retrying at another provider usually requires a new authentication. A second 3DS window is a common reason players abandon a deposit, so we decide when such a cascade is worth it based on amount, decline code and player history.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between routing and cascading?

Routing decides which provider a deposit goes to first. Cascading decides what happens after a decline: retry at another provider or not. They work together: good routing reduces declines, cascading recovers part of the rest.

How many providers do you need for cascading?

At least two for cards in each key geo. Ideally they sit on different acquirers: two PSPs on the same acquiring bank often decline the same way.

Can cascading hurt relationships with banks and card schemes?

Not if you follow Visa and Mastercard retry rules and never retry hard declines. Uncontrolled cascading, on the other hand, leads to fines and lowers banks’ trust in the merchant.

Do we need a payment orchestrator?

Not necessarily. Many gateways already have routing that just needs configuring. We don’t sell orchestrators: we compare options for your task, and the choice stays with you.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the task: reviewing current rules is part of an audit from €600; a full cycle with specs, configuration and support typically costs €3,000–4,000.

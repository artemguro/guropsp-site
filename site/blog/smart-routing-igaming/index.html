What Is Smart Payment Routing

Smart routing means every deposit goes to the provider where that particular card has the best chance of approval, and if it’s declined it automatically moves on. Here is how it differs from basic routing and cascading, and what it’s made of.

Routing: the basic rules

Routing is a set of rules the gateway uses to decide on the fly which provider a transaction goes to. A rule looks like “if conditions, then PSP X” and is built on attributes:

  • player GEO and currency;
  • payment method and amount;
  • card BIN: issuing bank, country, card type;
  • player status: first deposit (FTD) or repeat. Every provider has its own trusted-player criteria, and the rules are written to match them;
  • history: where the previous transaction went and how it ended.

Basic routing stops there: Germany, cards, FTD — to provider A. Smart routing adds data on how specific banks perform through specific providers and takes the player’s own history into account.

Routing solves three problems at once:

  • Conversion. The same BIN gets a different approval rate at different providers.
  • Cost. With comparable approval (within a few percentage points), the cheaper provider goes first and the expensive one lower or only in the cascade. The saving is tenths of a percent of volume or more.
  • Risk distribution. Volume is split between providers, so one outage doesn’t stop payment acceptance.

Cascading

A cascade automatically moves to the next provider if the first one declines the payment. The player doesn’t see the decline and doesn’t re-enter anything: one deposit gets several chances to go through.

Three principles of a working cascade:

  1. No delay. The switch should take seconds; it’s better not to show the player the intermediate error.
  2. No repeats. Each attempt goes through a different MID and, where possible, a different acquirer. Retrying the same channel almost always gets the same decline.
  3. Not everything is cascaded. Insufficient funds, an expired card, wrong card details, fraud suspicion raised by your own platform — these are declines about the player or the card. Retrying at another provider won’t fix them, and extra attempts worsen how banks and schemes view your traffic. Generic issuer declines and limits of a specific channel are what gets cascaded.

BIN routing

The BIN is the first 6–8 digits of a card. It shows the issuing bank, country and card type. Different banks approve payments differently through different providers, and BIN routing is built on that difference: the card goes straight to where its bank has the best approval rate.

Sticky routing

Sticky routing binds a player’s card to the provider that processed its last successful deposit. The next deposit with that card goes straight there, bypassing the general rules.

  • The binding lasts until the first unsuccessful transaction.
  • After a decline the binding is removed, and the card goes back through the general rules or to another provider.
  • That way the player doesn’t get “stuck” with a provider that has broken, and load balances itself.

Repeat deposits make up most of the volume, so sticky routing is one of the cheapest settings to implement and one of the most noticeable in effect.

In short

How is smart routing different from cascading?

Cascading gives a payment several attempts after a decline. Smart routing decides in what order and to which providers those attempts go, based on data about the card’s bank and the player’s history.

Where to start if there’s almost no routing?

With a cascade of at least two providers on different MIDs and sticky routing for repeat deposits. BIN routing comes once you’ve collected statistics by bank and provider.

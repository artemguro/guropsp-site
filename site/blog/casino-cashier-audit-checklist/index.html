Casino Cashier Audit: 10 Questions Worth Answering Honestly

A cashier can look fine and still lose a noticeable share of deposits. Answer the ten questions below. If the answer to even three of them is “no” or “don’t know”, your cashier is almost certainly losing money.

Contents

  1. 10 questions
  2. What the answers mean
  3. How an audit differs from a self-check

10 questions

  1. Do you know what share of registered players opens the cashier and what share makes a first deposit?
  2. For each key geo, does the cashier have the methods players there actually use?
  3. Does a repeat deposit take one or two clicks?
  4. Can you see how many players abandon 3DS — per provider?
  5. Do you have at least two card providers on different acquirers in each key geo?
  6. Does routing take the issuing bank into account rather than just splitting traffic by percentage?
  7. Are you sure your cascade doesn’t retry declines that must not be retried?
  8. Do you know each provider’s cost per successful deposit, not just its rate?
  9. Do you learn about approval rate drops from alerts before support tells you?
  10. Does approval rate have an owner who checks it every day?

What the answers mean

Each question is a separate area where deposits are lost quietly — invisible in the overall approval rate. “Don’t know” is worse than “no” here: it means that part of the funnel isn’t measured at all, and nobody is counting the losses.

How an audit differs from a self-check

A self-check shows where to look. In an audit we go through the deposit path ourselves, analyse the data by provider, method, geo and bank, estimate how much money is lost in each area, and come back with a prioritised plan of changes. We can go through these questions with you on an intro call — free of charge.

In short

How long does a self-check take?

A few minutes if you know the answers. If many answers are “don’t know”, that’s already a result: part of the funnel isn’t being measured.

How is a self-check different from a full audit?

A self-check shows where to look for problems. In an audit we analyse the data and settings ourselves, estimate the losses and deliver a prioritised plan of changes.

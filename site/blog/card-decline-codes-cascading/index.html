Card Decline Codes: Why You Can’t Retry Everything

Cascading recovers some deposits, but only if you understand why the bank declined. Retrying every decline means card scheme fees and damaged relationships with acquirers. Here is why there are no universal rules.

Contents

  1. Not all declines are equal
  2. Card schemes watch retries
  3. Cascading after 3DS is a separate story
  4. Why every matrix is different

Not all declines are equal

Declines fall into two broad groups. Soft — the bank may approve the payment later or at another acquirer. Hard — it won’t approve it under any conditions: for example, the card is lost or stolen. Retrying a hard decline won’t bring a deposit, but it will add pointless attempts on the card.

The trouble is that the line between them isn’t as obvious as it seems. The same code is interpreted differently by different acquirers and schemes, and some technically “soft” declines can’t be solved by cascading at all — the problem is the card, not the provider.

Card schemes watch retries

Visa limits the number of retries per card and prohibits retrying declines the issuer will never approve. Mastercard returns a Merchant Advice Code that states directly whether a retry is allowed. Merchants pay fees for violations, and the acquirer starts looking at the traffic more closely.

Rules and limits change from time to time, so a cascade set up “once and for all” gradually starts working against you.

Cascading after 3DS is a separate story

If a player has already passed 3DS and the provider declines the payment, a retry at another provider usually means a new authentication window. Some players leave at that point. Whether such a cascade is worth it depends on the amount, the decline code and the player’s history.

Why every matrix is different

A correct cascade matrix isn’t built from a table on the internet but from your own data: which declines, at which providers, for which banks and with what retry results. Two operators in the same geo can have fundamentally different matrices. And it needs regular review — providers and banks change their behaviour.

If your cascade was set up long ago or “by default”, it is probably losing deposits and creating extra risk at the same time. We review it on your data and build rules for your providers.

In short

What is payment cascading?

It is re-sending a declined transaction through another provider according to set rules in order to recover the deposit.

Can you retry every decline?

No. Hard declines must not be retried, and for the rest Visa and Mastercard limit the number of retries. Exceeding the limits leads to fees and fines.

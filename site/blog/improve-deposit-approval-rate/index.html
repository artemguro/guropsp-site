How to Improve Deposit Approval Rates in iGaming: 7 Levers

Deposit approval rate is one of the few metrics that turns straight into revenue: same traffic, same players, more successful payments. Here are the seven levers we start every payments audit with.

1. Measure approval rate properly

An average approval rate “for the project” tells you very little. The problem almost always sits in a specific segment: one provider, method, geo or issuing bank. So first break the metric down:

  • by provider and payment method;
  • by geo and currency;
  • by BIN and issuing bank;
  • by deposit amount;
  • by new versus returning players.

And always keep two numbers apart: attempt approval rate (how many transactions were approved) and player conversion (how many players who started a deposit actually completed one). Cascading and retries change the first a lot and barely show in the second — and the second is what makes money.

Also separate technical declines (timeouts, provider errors) from issuer declines. The first are fixed on your side and the provider’s, the second through routing, cascading and 3DS data.

2. Fix the cashier

Some deposits are lost before anything reaches the bank. Typical findings in a cashier audit:

  • a popular local method for a key geo is missing or buried at the bottom of the list;
  • poor default amounts and unnecessary fields;
  • saved cards aren’t offered on repeat deposits;
  • errors read “Something went wrong” with no hint of what to do next;
  • the mobile cashier is awkward, even though most traffic is mobile.

Each item looks minor, but together they noticeably cut player-to-deposit conversion.

3. Route by geo, BIN and issuing bank

The same issuing bank approves differently at different acquirers. If a geo’s entire volume goes to one “default” provider, you lose deposits wherever another provider performs better.

Smart routing picks the first provider for each transaction by geo, method, BIN, bank, amount and player history. Sticky routing works well: a returning player’s deposit goes where the previous one was approved.

4. Cascade soft declines only

Cascading retries a declined deposit at another provider. It recovers some payments, but only when tuned by decline code. Soft declines are worth retrying — generic issuer declines, issuer unavailable, system errors. Hard declines — lost or stolen card, suspected fraud, invalid card number — must not be retried.

Card schemes limit retries and fine merchants who exceed the limits, so uncontrolled cascading does harm. More in our article on decline codes.

5. Tune 3DS for conversion

In Europe strong customer authentication is mandatory for most card payments, and 3DS is one of the main points of loss. What helps:

  • send as much player and device data as possible in 3DS 2 — banks then approve more often without an extra challenge;
  • track the share of payments where the player abandoned the 3DS window, per provider;
  • plan cascading after 3DS carefully: a retry at another provider usually means a second challenge, and the player may leave.

6. Don’t keep all volume with one provider

A single provider for a key geo is a risk: any outage stops the cashier. You need at least two card providers, ideally on different acquirers. Plus automatic control: if a provider’s approval rate dips or technical errors rise, the router lowers its weight until it recovers.

A second provider also gives you leverage: terms, fees and reserves are easier to renegotiate when you have something to compare.

7. Build a decline handling process

Even the best setup degrades without a process. Make sure that:

  1. a specific person owns approval rate and checks it daily;
  2. alerts reach Telegram or Slack before player complaints do;
  3. declines are sorted by type: provider outage, cashier issue, anti-fraud, new unknown errors;
  4. escalations to providers come with specifics: time, segment, codes, example transactions;
  5. the effect of every change is measured against the baseline.

Where to start

Start with the first lever: break approval rate down by segment and find the weakest ones. Usually this step alone shows where the quick wins are — in the cashier, routing or a specific provider. In short

What is deposit approval rate?

It is the share of approved deposits out of all attempts. For the business, player conversion matters more: how many players who started a payment actually completed it.

What approval rate is considered normal?

There is no single benchmark: it depends heavily on geo, methods, traffic type and providers. It’s more useful to compare segments with each other and with your own numbers before changes.

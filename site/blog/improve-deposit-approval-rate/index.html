Deposit Approval Rates in iGaming: Where the Money Is Really Lost

Deposit approval rate is one of the few metrics that turns straight into revenue: same traffic, same players, more successful payments. Yet for almost every operator we’ve worked with, it’s lower than it could be. Here’s why.

Contents

  1. The average approval rate tells you almost nothing
  2. Where deposits get lost
  3. Why it’s hard to fix on your own
  4. Where to start

The average approval rate tells you almost nothing

“We’re at 80%” across the whole project is reassuring, but it hides the main point. Losses almost always sit in specific segments: one provider, one method, one geo or one issuing bank. On average things look fine, while in a single segment every third deposit fails.

The second trap is confusing attempt approval rate with player-to-deposit conversion. They’re different numbers, and the second one is what makes money. Cascades and retries move the first a lot and barely show in the second.

Where deposits get lost

Losses are spread along the whole payment path, and there are usually several at once:

  • Cashier. A missing method for the geo, a poor method order, unclear errors, an awkward mobile version.
  • Routing. A geo’s entire volume goes to one provider, even though another provider performs better for some banks.
  • Cascading. The wrong declines are retried, or in the wrong place — and that hurts how banks and card schemes view your traffic.
  • 3DS. Players abandon authentication, and the abandonment rate differs by provider.
  • Providers. One provider in a key geo, no backup, terms not revisited since launch.
  • Processes. Drops are noticed by support, not by alerts; escalations to providers lack specifics.

Why it’s hard to fix on your own

Each of these areas is connected to the others. Changing routing changes which declines reach the cascade. Cascading runs into Visa and Mastercard retry rules. A new provider changes the picture by issuing bank. And every change has to be measured against the baseline on the same segments — otherwise you can’t tell what worked.

That’s why spot fixes “by feel” often backfire: approval rate rises in one place and quietly drops in another. Raising it sustainably takes an analysis of the data across all segments and a prioritised plan of changes.

Where to start

If you can’t name your approval rate per provider, method and key bank off the top of your head, that’s the first sign money is being lost where you can’t see it. A payments audit starts with exactly that picture: we go through the entire deposit path, find where and how much is lost, and come back with a plan of changes.

In short

What is deposit approval rate?

It is the share of approved deposits out of all attempts. For the business, player conversion matters more: how many players who started a payment actually completed it.

What approval rate is considered normal?

There is no single benchmark: it depends heavily on geo, methods, traffic type and providers. It’s more useful to compare segments with each other and with your own numbers before changes.

How to Choose a PSP for iGaming: What to Understand Before Signing

A wrong PSP choice costs more than it seems during negotiations: lost deposits, frozen reserves, fines without evidence and months of re-integration. Here is where operators most often go wrong and why choosing a provider isn’t a rate comparison.

A PSP isn’t the first step

The most common mistake is starting with a provider “a friend already uses”. The cashier ends up with methods hardly anyone in your GEO pays with — and one provider for everything. The right choice starts with market and competitor analysis: first you know which methods you need, and only then who to get them from.

And you almost always need more than one provider. A single PSP means any outage, channel change or freeze takes payment acceptance to zero, and you have no leverage in negotiations.

“PSP” covers different businesses

Acquirer, aggregator and agent are three different models with different prices, requirements and different parties actually holding your money. Every intermediary in the chain adds margin and its own point of failure. And if two of your providers sit on the same acquirer, you effectively have no redundancy. You can’t see this from the outside — it has to be found out.

The rate in the offer is just one line

The real cost is made up of many fees, reserves, settlement terms and the conversion rate. Some of them only show up at a low approval rate or after the first dispute. That’s why providers are compared not by rate but by the cost of a successful deposit — and the PSP that’s cheapest on paper often turns out to be the most expensive.

The contract matters more than the deck

The most expensive problems — unilateral tariff changes, fines without evidence, reserve deductions and freeze terms — are written (or not written) into the contract and its annexes. Most operators only see them once money has already been held.

Technical details people forget

What data the provider returns on a decline, how statuses and callbacks work, how it separates first and repeat deposits and what it requires from your routing — this determines whether you can manage approval rates at all. A mistake here can mean not only lost deposits but a breach of contract.

Launch is part of the choice too

A sandbox isn’t production, and the real difference between providers only shows on live traffic and correctly chosen segments. Without that you’re comparing decks, not providers.

In short

How many PSPs do you need at launch?

More than one for each key method in your priority GEOs. The exact number depends on markets, methods and volumes.

What matters more — rate or approval rate?

Approval rate. A small difference in approval rate almost always outweighs a difference in rate, so compare the cost of a successful deposit.

GEO Analysis for iGaming Payments

A project’s payment set is defined by the market, not by the provider. A cashier that works in Europe will bring almost no deposits in Canada or Brazil. Here is how to analyse a GEO from a payments perspective and what you should end up with.

Why GEO comes first

The market drives almost everything in the payments setup: which methods you need, what share comes from cards, whether you need a local acquirer, and what rates, reserves and approval rates to expect. Providers should be sourced against a ready list of methods — otherwise the cashier is built from whatever was offered first.

The analysis is done separately for each priority GEO. Even neighbouring countries often run on different methods: iDEAL in the Netherlands, BLIK in Poland, cards and open banking in Scandinavia.

What to find out for each country

QuestionWhy it matters
Regulation and your licenceWhich methods are available in that country with your licence
How players payCards, open banking, wallets, vouchers, local transfers — and each method’s share of deposits
Cards and acquiringWhether cards go through international acquiring or need a local acquirer. In some countries cards are declined en masse without one
Pay-in and payoutOne-way methods (vouchers, cash) only accept deposits. Withdrawals need a separate channel
Provider riskFor GEOs the card schemes see as risky, providers more often ask for a security deposit, a higher reserve and watch chargebacks more closely
CurrenciesDeposits in local currency get approved more often than those converted on the bank’s side
Market complexityHow many providers actually work there, how stable they are and how much reserve you need to hold

Methods by region

A reference point to start from. For a specific country, always verify it against competitors’ cashiers.

RegionMain methodsComplexity

Plan payouts separately. For European traffic, SEPA Instant is usually several times cheaper than card payouts (OCT), settles in seconds and is declined less often on large amounts. Payout speed directly affects player retention.

Competitor benchmark

The most reliable way to see how a country pays is to look at what works for projects already earning there. Good starting points: casino directories by country (for example, the countries section on AskGamblers), Similarweb for traffic and top GEOs, Blask for regulated markets, Ahrefs for finding affiliates and review sites.

Who to include in the sample:

  1. The same target GEO. Method sets differ a lot between countries.
  2. The same licence type. Some methods aren’t available to every licence, and acquiring terms differ fundamentally.
  3. A similar product. White label and an own platform give different cashier capabilities.
  4. Comparable age. Large older projects often have individual deals and their own providers.
  5. Active marketing. If a project’s traffic is falling, its cashier is probably outdated.

The sweet spot is 5–10 projects. Fewer than five and you won’t see patterns; more than ten and the analysis drags on for weeks and loses focus.

How to snapshot a cashier and find providers

  1. Sign up and deposit. Only then do you see the real cashier with live methods, not a row of logos in the footer.
  2. Cashier snapshot. Screenshots of all methods with display order, limits, currencies and fees. Check on different devices: on mobile the set and order of methods often differ.
  3. Redirects. During a deposit the payment passes through the gateway’s and provider’s domains. You can see them in the DevTools Network tab or on a screen recording. The domain identifies the provider, and Similarweb (outgoing links) shows other projects working with it.
  4. Withdrawals. Which payout methods are available, minimums, fees and speed. The payout set often differs from the deposit set.
  5. Summary table. Project × method × currency × limits × provider. This is the working document for the decision.

A provider domain you’ve found is a direct way into negotiations. Providers’ BD managers can be found on LinkedIn: a short four-line message with the GEO, the method you need and expected volume works better than a long email.

What you should end up with

The result isn’t a presentation but a document you can use straight away to build the cashier and source providers:

  • A list of priority GEOs with licence restrictions and a complexity rating.
  • A method map for each country: pay-in and payout, priority, share of deposits at competitors.
  • Method order in the cashier: the most popular method in the country first.
  • A benchmark of minimum and maximum amounts per method, so limits match the market.
  • A list of providers found at competitors, with BD contacts.
  • A prioritised method wishlist to take into PSP sourcing: at least two providers per key method.

In short

Can one cashier serve every country?

Technically yes, but conversion will be lower. The cashier is tuned per GEO: the most common method in the country goes first and irrelevant methods are hidden.

How long does analysing one GEO take?

With a sample of 5–10 competitors, usually a few working days; most of the time goes on sign-ups, deposits and tracing redirects.
